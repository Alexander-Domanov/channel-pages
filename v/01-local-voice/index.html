A voice for your videos, free

Record ten seconds of your voice once. Every video after that narrates itself in your voice, and you stop paying for it.

What you need:

  • A Mac, Windows, or Linux computer.
  • Python 3.12 or newer.
  • A quiet room and ten seconds of your voice.
  • Your script as a text file.
  • A few gigabytes free for the model files.

Why bother

Three videos a week, twenty minutes of talking in each. In subscriptions that runs $22 to $35 a month.

Your laptop does the same job. You write the next script while it talks.

Words you'll meet

Five words turn up in every guide. None of them are hard.

WordWhat it means here
SampleA short recording of you talking. Three to ten seconds.
CloneThe program copies how you sound. It never records you again.
ModelThe part that turns text into speech. It downloads once.
EnvironmentA folder where one program keeps its parts. Two programs, two folders.
LocalIt runs on your laptop. Nothing goes to a server.

Two programs, both free

OmniVoice is the fast one. It comes from k2-fsa, an open research team. It knows 646 languages, Spanish among them. In testing, twenty-two seconds of speech came back in twenty-six. github.com/k2-fsa/OmniVoice

Qwen3-TTS comes from the Qwen team at Alibaba. It knows ten languages, Spanish among them. The same twenty-two seconds took seventy-three. github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS

Start with OmniVoice. Twenty minutes of narration takes it about twenty-three minutes. The other one takes an hour. Keep Qwen3-TTS for a second voice, to compare a line that sounds off.

Both are open source under the Apache 2.0 licence. You can sell what you make.

Install

The setup is a few commands. The one part worth your full attention: the two programs go in separate folders.

  1. Install Python once. Both programs are written in it. Get version 3.12 or newer from python.org.
  2. Make one folder for each program. This is the step people skip. The two programs need different versions of the same shared piece. In one folder they break each other.
  3. In the OmniVoice folder, run two lines. The first installs the maths part, the second installs the program. 
    pip install torch==2.8.0 torchaudio==2.8.0
pip install omnivoice
    On Windows or Linux with an NVIDIA card, the first line is different. The OmniVoice page has it, under Installation.
  4. Qwen3-TTS, in its own folder. 
    pip install -U qwen-tts
    Its model files download the first time you run it. That's the slow part.
  5. Point the program at your sample and your script. Press start. Both projects show the exact line on their own page. OmniVoice also opens a window you can click: omnivoice-demo --port 8001.

Your sample

That recording decides how every video after it sounds. It takes a minute.

  • Three to ten seconds. Ten is a good starting point.
  • Qwen3-TTS clones from a three-second sample, per its own docs. Longer clips slow both programs down and can sound worse.
  • Read something neutral. Not a list of instructions — one test recording read the instructions aloud.
  • Read at the pace you want to be listened to. The voice copies your speed.
  • Record in the language you want it to sound native in. A Russian sample gives a Russian accent everywhere else.

The sample page has the full list of eight rules and the numbers behind them.

What trips people up

  • The two programs fight. Separate folders fix it. In one folder, both stop working.
  • A voice can't be described in words. The programs take gender, age, pitch, accent and whisper. That's all.
  • In clone mode, the accent comes from your sample. A Russian sample gives a Russian accent. For a native Spanish voice, record the sample in Spanish.
  • Keep the sample file. When a program updates, you rebuild the voice from it. Nothing else remembers your voice.

What it can't do

  • It sounds good. Not "good for a robot", just good. It still isn't a person. For a big brand's ad, hire a person.
  • Your own clone is fine by YouTube's rules. Their own list of examples puts "cloning one's own voice to create voice overs" under things you don't have to disclose. Someone else's voice is the opposite. Get their permission first. A realistic clone of a real person is exactly what YouTube asks you to disclose. Rules change, so check the current page.
