A voice for your videos, free

Record ten seconds of your voice once. Every video after that narrates itself in your voice, and you stop paying for it.

Why bother

Three videos a week, twenty minutes of talking in each. In subscriptions that runs $22 to $35 a month. Your laptop does the same job while you write the next script.

How to install

You copy two commands and press enter. That's the whole job.

  1. Install Python once. Both programs are written in it. Get it from python.org, version 3.12 or newer.
  2. Make one folder for each program. This is the step people skip. OmniVoice wants a newer shared piece than Qwen3-TTS does. In one folder they fight. In two, they don't touch.
  3. In the OmniVoice folder, run two lines. The first installs the maths part, the second installs the program. 
    pip install torch==2.8.0 torchaudio==2.8.0
pip install omnivoice
    On Windows or Linux with an NVIDIA card, the first line is different. The OmniVoice page has it, under Installation.
  4. Qwen3-TTS, in its own folder. 
    pip install -U qwen-tts
    Its model files download by themselves the first time you run it.
  5. Point the program at your sample and your script. Press start. Both projects show the exact line on their own page, and both have a window you can click instead of type.

Go slowly and it's two evenings. The setup page lists the four things that go wrong, and what to do about each.

What you do

  1. Record ten seconds of yourself talking, in a quiet room. One take.
  2. Install the program, in its own folder.
  3. Paste your script in. Press start. Walk away.
  4. Come back to a finished audio file and drop it into your editor.

Do the steps in order. Don't skip the first one.

That recording decides how every video after it sounds, and it's the step people rush. Twenty minutes of narration takes about twenty minutes to make. A laptop with eight gigabytes of memory handles it. Nothing here needs an expensive computer.

What trips people up

  • The two programs fight. Each needs a different version of the same shared piece. Put them in separate folders. Together, both stop working and you'll blame yourself.
  • The accent comes from your recording. Read in Russian and your Spanish sounds Russian. Want Mexican Spanish? Record a Spanish sample, eight or nine seconds.
  • You can't describe a voice in words. The programs take a voice from gender, age, pitch, accent and whisper. Nothing else. That's why the sample beats every setting.
  • Your pace carries over. Read the sample the way you want to be listened to.

What it can't do

  • It sounds good. Not "good for a robot", just good. It still isn't a person. For a big brand's ad, hire a person.
  • Your own voice is yours. Someone else's needs their permission. On YouTube, a cloned voice that isn't yours also needs the AI label. With your own, there's nothing to flag.
