A voice for your videos, free

Record ten seconds of your voice once. After that every video you make gets narrated in your voice, and you stop paying a monthly fee for it.

Why bother

Three videos a week with twenty minutes of talking in each one costs between $264 and $420 a year in subscriptions. Your laptop does the same job in about the time it takes you to write the next script.

Words you'll meet

Five words show up in every guide, and none of them are hard.

How to install

You won't be writing code. You copy two commands, press enter, and wait for the download.

  1. Install Python once. Both programs are written in it. Get it from python.org, version 3.12 or newer.
  2. Make one folder for each program. This is the step people skip, and it's the one that costs them an evening. OmniVoice wants a newer version of a shared piece than Qwen3-TTS does. In one folder they fight; in two they don't touch.
  3. In the OmniVoice folder, run these two lines. The first one installs the part that does the maths, the second installs the program itself. 
    pip install torch==2.8.0 torchaudio==2.8.0
pip install omnivoice
    On a Windows or Linux machine with an NVIDIA card the first line is different — the exact version is on the OmniVoice page, under Installation.
  4. Qwen3-TTS, in its own folder: 
    pip install -U qwen-tts
    Its model files download by themselves the first time you run it.
  5. Point the program at your sample and your script, then press start. Both projects show the exact line on their own page, and both have a window you can open locally instead of typing anything.

The whole setup is two evenings if you go slowly, and one if you don't. The setup page has the four things that go wrong and what to do about them.

What you do

  1. Find a quiet room and record ten seconds of yourself talking. One take is enough.
  2. Install the program, in its own folder.
  3. Paste your script in, press start, walk away.
  4. Come back to a finished audio file and drop it into your video editor.

Do the steps in order, and don't skip the first one. That ten-second recording decides how every video after it sounds, and it's the step people rush.

Twenty minutes of narration takes about twenty minutes to make, and the next script can wait in another window while it works. A laptop with eight gigabytes of memory handles this. Nothing here needs an expensive computer.

What trips people up

  • The two programs fight each other. OmniVoice needs a newer version of the same shared piece than Qwen3-TTS does. Put them in two separate folders. Keep them together and both stop working, and you'll spend the evening sure you did something wrong. You didn't. Move them apart.
  • The accent comes from your recording, not from the language. Record in Russian and your Spanish will sound Russian. Want your Spanish to sound Mexican? Record a Spanish sample, eight or nine seconds is plenty.
  • You can't describe a voice in words. Both programs take a voice from these attributes only: gender, age, pitch, accent, whisper. A recording beats a description, which is why the ten-second sample matters more than any setting.
  • Slow and calm stays slow and calm. However you read the sample is how every future video will sound. Read it the way you want to be listened to.

What it can't do

  • It sounds good. Not "good for a robot", just good. But it isn't a person, and for a big brand's ad, hire a person.
  • Your own voice is yours to use. Someone else's needs their permission, and if you use theirs YouTube also makes you flag the video as AI. With your own voice there's nothing to flag.
