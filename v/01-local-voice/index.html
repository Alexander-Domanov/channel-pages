What I did instead

I put two open models on a MacBook with an M2 chip and 8 GB of memory. No NVIDIA card in sight, the Apple graphics cores do the math. Both models are Apache 2.0, and that matters for exactly one reason: you're allowed to make money with what they produce.

The part nobody warns you about

The two models can't live in the same place. One needs library version 5.3 or newer, the other is pinned to 4.57. Install them together and neither one works. Keep two separate environments and you'll never think about it again.

The voice

Both models clone a voice from a 3–10 second sample. Record it once, keep the file, and it narrates every video after that. Two things follow from that, and both bit me:

  • The accent comes from the sample, not from the language. A sample in Russian gives you Russian-accented Spanish. Want Mexican Spanish? Record a Spanish sample, about 8–9 seconds.
  • You can't describe a voice in words. The model takes a fixed list: gender, age, pitch, accent, whisper. That's it. Cloning is easier than inventing.

Where it falls short

  • It sounds good. Not "good for a robot" good, just good. But it isn't a person. On YouTube you won't hear the difference; in a big brand's ad you will, and there I'd still hire the human.
  • If you clone your own voice, YouTube doesn't ask you to flag the video as AI. Clone someone else's and it does, and you also need their permission.
