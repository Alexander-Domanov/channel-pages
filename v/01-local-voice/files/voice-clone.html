Recording your sample

Ten seconds decide how every video after it sounds. It takes a minute, and it's the step people rush.

Do this after the program has already spoken once with its own voice — step 5 on the setup page. Check the install first, record second.

How to record it

  1. Use the microphone you have. A phone or a laptop is fine. Don't buy one for this.
  2. Pick the quietest room. Soft things around you help: a bed, a couch, a wardrobe behind you. No music, no fan, no open window.
  3. Aim for eight or nine seconds. Anything under six comes out rushed: a five-and-a-half second sample ran at 227 words a minute. Past nine seconds, nothing changes.
  4. Read anything neutral. A paragraph from a book, a line from a news story. Not a list of instructions — one test recording picked up the instruction text and read it aloud.
  5. Read at the pace you want to be listened to. A sample read calmly came out at 153 words a minute, and every video after it kept that pace. The voice copies your speed.
  6. Record in the language you want to sound native in. A Russian sample gives you a Russian accent everywhere else. For a Mexican Spanish channel, read Spanish, eight or nine seconds.
  7. One take. Don't polish it. The program wants how you sound, not a performance.
  8. Keep the file. When the program updates, you rebuild the voice from this sample. Nothing else remembers it.

What you can't do

You can't describe a voice in words. "Warm, confident, documentary" gets you nowhere.

Both programs take a voice from five things: gender, age, pitch, accent, whisper. That's why a ten-second recording beats an hour of settings.
