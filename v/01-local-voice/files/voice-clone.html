Recording your sample

Ten seconds decide how your channel sounds from now on. Record it once, keep the file, never think about it again.

How to do it

  1. Sit in the quietest room you have. Close the window, turn off the fan.
  2. Record ten seconds of yourself talking the way you want your videos to sound.
  3. Listen to it once. If you hear wind, traffic or a fridge, record it again.
  4. Save the file somewhere you'll still find it in a year, and put a copy in the cloud.

Why it matters this much

  • The program copies, it doesn't invent. There's no box where you type "warm male voice, slight rasp". You hand it a recording and it does that voice forever.
  • Your accent comes along. Whatever accent you record is the accent you get, even when the words are in another language. Record in Russian and your Spanish sounds Russian. Want Spanish? Record a Spanish sample, eight or nine seconds is enough.
  • Your pace comes along too. Read slowly and every video after that is calm. Race through it and every video sounds rushed. Pick the pace you'd want for an hour-long tutorial, not for a highlight reel.
  • Noise comes along as well. A recording taken next to a fan gives you a faint fan in every video.

Two languages, two files

If you're publishing in English and Spanish, record both samples in the same sitting while the house is quiet. Same voice, two languages, one afternoon.

Your voice stays yours. The file doesn't leave your computer, and nobody needs to hear it to use the program.
